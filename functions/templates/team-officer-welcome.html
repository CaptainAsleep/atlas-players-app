1
Decide who gets in

In your team settings, choose whether anyone can join or whether officers approve each request. New teams start on approval. Requests show up in a Pending Requests list on your team page, where you can Approve or Deny each one. You can also remove a member from the roster at any time.

2
Bring in more officers

Any member can ask to become an officer, and any current officer can approve or deny the request. You can also promote someone straight from the roster. Every officer has the same powers, including the person who created the team.

3
Post your team's events

Add events to your team calendar with dates, a banner, your OPORD link, a ticket link, and a location. If the event is already running on Atlas, link it — the card then pulls the live name, date, field, banner and price automatically. Members RSVP going, interested or not going, and you see the full list. Every event has a Share button, so you can drop the invite into Discord or your group text. One heads-up: your team page is public, so an OPORD link you attach is visible to anyone who opens it.

4
Build your rank ladder

Create your own ranks with a name, an optional insignia image, and what it takes to earn the rank. Move them up and down to set the order, then assign ranks from the roster. Everyone can see the ladder, so members know what they’re working toward.

5
Create custom roles

Make your own role titles — Medic, Squad Leader, Recon, whatever your team runs — each with a short description. Members can hold more than one. Roles are labels only: they show on the roster but don’t change what anyone is allowed to do.

6
Put your team on the map

Set a home field, upload your team patch, and write your team description. Add links to your Discord, website, Facebook, Instagram and YouTube, plus one custom link of your own, so people who find you on Atlas can find you everywhere else too.

Your team page is ready
Open Atlas, go to the Social tab, and tap your team. Everything above is on that one page — officers see the edit controls, everyone else sees the result.
